Why it stalls

Good products don’t fail on technology. They fail in translation.

You talk about your product. The owner asks about their building, their tenants and their risk.

Your solution
Your pitch saysOur system generates 400 MWh of electricity a year.
Asset management asksRoof lease or system lease? And how much of that reaches my NOI?

How to win them over: with the model that fits the building: income for the owner, a term that matches the holding period, the effect on energy class and value.

Bring on board: Property management (billing) · Facility management (roof, operations) · Tenants (on-site tenant electricity, power price)

Your pitch saysNo upfront investment – we take care of the heat supply.
Asset management asksWe’re selling in five years. Does the contract transfer, and what do tenants end up paying?

How to win them over: with a term that matches the holding period, a clean transfer on sale and a clear answer on costs for tenants.

Bring on board: Property management (service charges) · Facility management (heating, maintenance) · Tenants (costs, comfort)

Your pitch saysRemotely readable and fully compliant.
Asset management asksThat’s a legal requirement and everyone offers it. What do I get beyond compliance?

How to win them over: with data that does more than billing: spot leaks early, manage consumption, deliver the figures banks and investors ask for.

Bring on board: Property management (billing) · Facility management (installation) · Tenants (transparency, data protection)

Your pitch saysOur energy management software saves 30%.
Asset management asksWho measures that independently? And if the tenant saves: can I pass the running costs on?

How to win them over: with independent measurement, a clear answer on cost pass-through and a plan for bringing tenants and on-site technical staff along.

Bring on board: Property management (service charges) · Facility management (technology, IT security) · Tenants (comfort, costs)

Your pitch saysAll the ESG data for your portfolio at the push of a button.
Fund management asksWhich data do I actually need, and for whom: the bank, investors or the regulator?

How to win them over: by showing which specific requirement you meet and how much work you take off fund management’s plate.

Bring on board: Asset management (data per building) · Property management (consumption data) · Tenants (data consent)

Your pitch saysCharging infrastructure for every parking space.
Asset management asksWho operates it, who is liable, and what does it do for my building?

How to win them over: with a clear operating model and the benefit for the building: what tenants need, which obligations are met, what income it generates.

Bring on board: Property management (usage, billing) · Facility management (operations, maintenance) · Tenants (demand, costs)

From practice

Why I turned down proposals as a decision-maker

From my time on the owner’s side, anonymised.

The promise was too good to be true.

We were shown the savings from the best projects. What comparable buildings achieve on average remained unclear.

What would have convinced me: realistic average values from comparable use cases instead of the best individual cases.

The tenant question went unanswered.

Nobody could say whether the running costs could be passed on to tenants. Without that answer, nothing adds up for the owner.

What would have convinced me: a clear answer on cost pass-through and a fallback if the costs can’t be passed on.

The contract outlasted the anchor tenant’s lease.

The proposal came with a long contract term. At that point, nobody knew whether the anchor tenant would stay or whether we would hold or sell the property. The contract had no answer for either.

What would have convinced me: a term that fits the leases, or clear rules for exit and transfer on a sale.
